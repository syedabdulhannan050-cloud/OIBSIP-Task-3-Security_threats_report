## 1. Introduction

Network security protects computers, servers, networks, and information from unauthorized access and malicious activity. Common network threats can interrupt online services, expose sensitive information, or misrepresent the source of network traffic.

This report explains three common threats: Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS), Man-in-the-Middle (MITM), and IP spoofing. It also discusses their effects and basic prevention methods.

## 2. Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)

### Definition

A Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attack occurs when an attacker uses many devices or sources to send excessive traffic to a target server or service. The traffic may consume available resources and make the service slow or unavailable to legitimate users.

### How it works

1. An attacker directs traffic toward a target service.
2. Traffic from many sources reaches the target.
3. The target struggles to handle the excessive requests or packets.
4. Genuine users may experience delays or lose access to the service.

Attackers may use a group of compromised devices called a **botnet**.

### Impact

- Websites or online services may become unavailable.
- Organizations may lose time and money.
- Customers may be unable to access important services.
- An organization’s reputation may be affected.

### Prevention and mitigation

- Use suitable DDoS protection services.
- Monitor traffic for unusual increases.
- Apply rate limiting and traffic filtering where appropriate.
- Coordinate with hosting providers and internet service providers during an attack.
- Maintain an incident response plan.

A basic firewall can help enforce traffic rules, but a host firewall alone cannot guarantee protection from every DDoS attack.

## 3. Man-in-the-Middle (MITM)

### Definition

A Man-in-the-Middle attack occurs when an attacker positions themselves between two communicating parties and attempts to intercept or modify their communication.

### How it works

1. A user connects to a network or online service.
2. An attacker gains a position that allows them to interfere with the communication.
3. The attacker may attempt to observe or modify data.
4. The success of the attack depends on the network setup and security protections in place.

For example, a malicious Wi-Fi access point may be used to attempt to intercept a user's traffic.

### Impact

- Sensitive information may be exposed if communication is not adequately protected.
- Data may be altered in some circumstances.
- Users may be redirected to malicious websites.
- Stolen information may be misused.

### Prevention

- Use HTTPS websites and secure communication protocols.
- Verify Wi-Fi network names before connecting.
- Avoid sensitive transactions on untrusted networks.
- Keep browsers and operating systems updated.
- Pay attention to certificate warnings.
- Use a trusted VPN when appropriate.

HTTPS provides important protection for browser-to-website communication, but no single measure guarantees protection against every possible MITM scenario.

## 4. IP Spoofing

### Definition

IP spoofing is a technique in which the source IP address in a network packet is falsified, making the packet appear to come from a different address.

### How it works

1. A sender creates a network packet.
2. The source IP address is falsified.
3. The packet is sent toward a destination.
4. The destination sees the claimed source address, which may not identify the real sender.

Spoofing a source address does not automatically provide access to a victim's computer or account.

### Impact

- It can make identifying the real source of traffic more difficult.
- It can be used in some denial-of-service attacks.
- It may misdirect replies in certain attack scenarios.
- It can complicate network investigation.

### Prevention

- Apply ingress and egress filtering at suitable network boundaries.
- Use firewall and router rules to reject invalid source addresses.
- Monitor unusual traffic patterns.
- Use authentication rather than trusting an IP address alone.
- Keep network devices properly configured and updated.

Ingress filtering examines traffic entering a network, while egress filtering examines traffic leaving it. Both can help reduce IP spoofing.

## 5. Comparison of the Three Threats

| Threat | Main idea | Possible effect | Example protection |
|---|---|---|---|
| DDoS | Overwhelming a service with traffic from many sources | Service disruption | DDoS mitigation and traffic filtering |
| MITM | Intercepting or modifying communication | Data exposure or alteration | HTTPS and secure network practices |
| IP spoofing | Falsifying a packet's source IP address | Misleading traffic origin or supporting attacks | Ingress and egress filtering |

## 6. Role of Firewalls and Network Monitoring

Firewalls help control network traffic according to configured rules. Network monitoring helps administrators identify unusual activity and investigate potential incidents.

Tools such as Nmap can help authorized administrators discover hosts, open ports, and exposed services. UFW can apply firewall rules on supported Linux systems.

However, Nmap is a discovery and scanning tool, not a complete defense against these threats. UFW also does not independently prevent every MITM, spoofing, or DDoS scenario. Effective security uses multiple appropriate controls.

## 7. Conclusion

DDoS, MITM, and IP spoofing are different network security threats. DDoS primarily affects service availability, MITM targets communication, and IP spoofing falsifies the claimed source address of packets.

Organizations can reduce risk through appropriate filtering, secure communication, monitoring, updates, and incident response planning. Understanding these threats is an important first step toward learning network security.
